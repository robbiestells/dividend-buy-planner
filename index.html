When do prices update?

ETFs and stocks (JEPQ, QQQI, MLPI and the like) trade all day while the market is open, 9:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. Eastern on weekdays. The closing price is set at 4 p.m.

Mutual funds (like FSPTX) have one price a day, the net asset value, calculated after the close and usually posted by about 6 p.m. Eastern.

Distributions are announced by each fund, usually a few days before the ex-dividend date. Checking pulls the last year of payouts and their ex-dates for monthly payers.

The planner doesn't update on its own. Tap Check for new prices before a buy; it fetches the latest price, 52-week range and payouts for every fund. Needs sign-in. You can always type values in by hand below.

How to play

How the score works

Total return blends where the price sits in its 52-week range (lower is cheaper), whether recent distributions are rising or shrinking, and a sustainability check that discounts yields far above 12%, since those usually come partly out of NAV.

Monthly dividend rewards a higher yield on the price you pay, with a small bonus when the next ex-dividend date lands 1 to 10 days after the buy.

Balance favors sectors below their target and, within a sector, funds below their share of it. The order is built one share at a time, re-scoring after each share, so cash spreads out.

Total return counts the per-fund distribution log (the planner adds an estimate when each ex-dividend date passes; confirm it with the real amount) plus anything entered as received before tracking began. This is a rules-based helper, not financial advice; ex-dividend dates are estimates, and selling can create taxes.
